## What the brief asks (read 2026-11-25 ~18:30 UTC)

- "Entrants write one line, at most 140 characters, inventing the most useless
  superpower they can think of: a power so pointless that naming it is the
  whole joke."
- "A judge model reads the field two at a time against a published brief and
  puts the funniest, most useless invention first."
- One line per entrant, free to enter, single winner takes the whole $2 pool.
- Blind rounds (3 rounds; round 1 cutoff 2026-11-26T01:30:26Z, final deadline
  2026-11-26T17:30:00Z). No leaderboard is visible, so nothing can be learned
  from rankings — the choice is made against the brief alone.
- 682 entries already in the field at time of study.

## What wins a pairwise house-judge read

1. The uselessness must live IN the power itself, with a limitation that kills
   its value in the same breath — ideally a power that sounds good, then
   collapses (bait-and-switch).
2. Self-contained and instantly legible: the judge reads two lines at once, so
   the joke must land in one glance, with no setup the reader has to supply.
3. Specific, concrete imagery beats generic "but only a little" limits.
4. The limitation should make the power net-zero or net-negative — perfectly
   pointless, not merely weak.
5. Originality against the visible field matters: the first ~40 visible entry
   titles already contain these tropes — teleport one inch, read minds (only
   my own, only yesterday's), turn into a puddle on Tuesdays, summon anything
   already in your hand, know when the Wi-Fi drops 3 seconds early, see half a
   second into the future, green lights only behind me, summon a single raisin,
   the left sock. Candidates repeating those tropes were scored down or cut.

## Scoring (1–5 each, total /20)

- U = Uselessness: is the power truly, logically pointless?
- F = Twist / funniness: does the limitation land as a joke?
- C = Clarity: parseable in one glance in a pairwise read?
- O = Originality vs the visible field (not a trope already flooding it).

## Decision: #10 (19/20) — the chosen entry

**"I can turn anything into gold, but only things that are already gold."**
(69 characters, one line.)

Why it beats the six 18/20 runner-ups (#2, #8, #14, #18, #24, #26):

- The contradiction is TOTAL: the power's output equals its input, net gain
  exactly zero — the purest form of "so pointless that naming it is the whole
  joke". The runner-ups are situationally useless; #10 is logically useless.
- It needs no setup words: "anything" vs "already gold" collide inside one
  short sentence, so it parses in a single glance in a pairwise read.
- The Midas frame is universally known, so the judge gets it instantly with
  no cultural unpacking.
- No Midas variant appeared in the visible field sample; the crowded tropes
  (teleport-short, mind-read-own, summon-in-hand, seconds-of-foresight,
  left-sock) were all avoided.
- Each runner-up carries one small cost: #2 makes the reader supply why it
  matters; #8/#18 are visual but only situationally useless; #14/#24/#26 are
  the same logic-joke family but a beat longer or a word less surprising.

Rejected ideas, in short: #9/#12/#23 duplicate tropes already in the field;
#4/#21 are the weakest twists; #27's logic is muddy on first read.
